The Winter Larder

Cave bear den · in the bluff, half a day south of Stonetop · deep winter · 8 areas · 4 Surplus

Cave Bear · Bear of Winter · Debts and promises · Hec’tumel Codex

Truth

A bull and a sow sleep in a cave in the bluff south of the village. They have not foraged in weeks because they cannot: a Fae of the Bear of Winter’s retinue has frosted them under and is using the back of their cave as a larder for her master’s meat. The Bear’s domain opens onto any cave mouth ringed in snowdrifts, and this winter it opens here.

The Fae is Hush-of-Snow. She carries the Hec’tumel Codex, given to her freely, and what is freely given cannot be given back. Hec’tumel talks to her through it every night. She is thinner than she was. If the PCs are the ones who gave it to her, she is glad to see them; if not, she has heard of them, and she wants to know how a mortal stops hearing it.

The meat is not hers. A rival in the retinue, Pale-as-Milk, is watching to catch her giving it away. The pressure here is debt. Nobody has to fight anything unless they choose to, and the worst thing that can happen is that somebody agrees to something.

“The snow is banked in a clean ring around the cave mouth, waist-high, and there is not one track in it. Not out, not in. Not a bird’s.”

What the village needs

Run this when winter’s Seasons Change comes up short: on a 7-9 the steading must consume 1d4+Population more Surplus before winter ends or Meet with Disaster. A butchered cave bear is 1 Surplus. The larder at the back of the cave holds 4. Each Surplus is one sled home.

If they come home short, the player picks the consequence: Population −1, a resource lost (the Cistern, a horse), an important NPC dies, or a PC leaves play.

Travel — Chart a Course

Player-facing. Only what they know when they draw it up. The book’s own card for this trip, with winter weather.

  • ☐ It’ll take at least a day there and back; little need to worry about supplies
  • ☐ Bring a sled for every carcass you mean to haul home
  • ☐ Bring warm clothes
  • ☐ You need to watch out for crinwin, and for wolves working the bluff in the cold
  • ☐ The way is grueling: cold, snow, the sleds
  • ☐ You risk drawing the attention of the cave bears as you approach

Following the bluff south, they cannot get lost. If someone asks about the ring of drifts before they leave, the hunters know the den and know bears don’t make a ring. Nobody in the village knows what does.

1. The Drifts

Approach
“The bluff bends and there is the cave, a black mouth under an overhang, and the snow is banked round it in a ring as neat as a kerb. Nothing has crossed it. The cold here is a different cold; it sits on the skin and waits.”
  • A hunter reads it for free: no bear has left this cave in weeks. No scat, no scrape, no dig. They should be dead of hunger, or not here.
  • Cross the ring quietly: Defy Danger +DEX for the group, once. Miss: the snow squeals underfoot and the first sleeper (3) is stirring when they reach it.
  • Cross with sleds: the sleds stay outside the ring, or they come in and the roll is made with disadvantage.
  • Iron: ask what they brought. Village spears are iron or bronze; say which. Nothing in here minds bronze.
“Inside the mouth the snow stops as if cut with a knife. Beyond it, a dead fire and a man sitting up beside it.”

2. The Poacher’s Camp

Sign Loot
“A Marshedge trapper, by the cut of his coat, sitting upright by a fire that went out a long time ago. Frost on his eyelashes. His pack is full of meat, cut and wrapped, and nobody has touched it.”
  • The pack: provisions (◇◇◇◇◇◇ uses), cut from a bear. He got one, and then he got no further. Not enough for a Surplus; enough to feed the party and the sled-dogs.
  • The trap: an iron leg-trap, ◇, set and sprung on nothing. Iron hurts Fae without a hand on it (Armor 0 vs iron). Seed it here; let them work out later what it is for.
  • How he died: Know Things. Not cold alone. He sat down by a lit fire and did not get up, and the fire went out around him. Someone offered him something and he took it.
  • His face: a Marshedge man in the village’s hunting ground, in winter, a long way from the Fen road. If your Marshedge owes the party a thread, he is it. Otherwise he is a warning.
“Past the camp the cave bends and the floor is scuffed smooth. Something very large sleeps in there. Two somethings.”

3. The Sleepers

Fight if woken 1 Surplus each
“A bull and a sow, each the size of a cart, curled against the cave wall. They are rimed with frost, thick as fur, and the frost does not melt in the heat of your breath. Their ribs rise and fall very, very slowly.”

Cave Bear (bull)

Solitary, large, cautious

HP 16; Armor 1 (fur, hide)
Damage claws, bite, hug (hand, close, forceful, messy, 1 piercing)
Special qualities frosted under; wakes at a touch, a shout, or open flame within reach
Instinct to fill its belly, to protect its young

  • Rend, maul, crush
  • Move with surprising speed and grace
  • Sniff out food, or trouble

Cave Bear (sow)

Solitary, large, cautious

HP 16; Armor 1 (fur, hide)
Damage claws, bite, hug (hand, close, forceful, messy, 1 piercing)
Special qualities frosted under; wakes if the cub (4) is handled roughly or cries out
Instinct to fill its belly, to protect its young

  • Sneak past: Defy Danger +DEX, each PC. Miss: a sleeper’s eye opens. It is slow for one exchange (the frost), then it is a bear.
  • Kill one in its sleep: no roll to start; a spear through the frost into the throat. It takes a full exchange to die and it does not die quietly: the other wakes. One carcass is 1 Surplus, a sled, and an hour’s butchering in the cold.
  • Open flame: the frost is Fae-work and fire undoes it. A torch or a lit Flaming Sword within reach of a sleeper runs the rime off and the bear is awake and hungry. Fire is also the only light past the camp; say so before anyone strikes one.
  • The frost that doesn’t melt: Know Things, or a Ranger’s wood-sense: this is glamour, not weather. Something is keeping them under on purpose.
“Something small moves at the edge of the light, low to the ground, and it is not frosted at all.”

4. The Cub

Beat
“A yearling cub, thin as a dog, awake. It has been eating what it could reach from the trapper’s pack. It looks at whoever smells most of meat the way a dog looks at the one person in the room with a bone.”
  • It follows that PC. Quietly, at a distance, through the rest of the cave. It is not a threat and nothing in the cave treats it as one.
  • Handle it roughly, or let it cry out: the sow wakes (3), frost or no frost.
  • Feed it: it stays. A cub a hunter raises is a follower (Book I, Followers): cub, keen-nosed, strong, clumsy; HP 6; Damage (hand, forceful); Instinct to follow food; Cost meat, and warmth. It will be a bear in three years. If a player has asked how pets work, this is how.
  • The question it raises: if they kill the sow for a Surplus, the cub is theirs to feed or to leave. Don’t decide for them. Make sure they know it is a choice.
“Past the sleepers the cave should end. It does not. The walls go on, smooth and cold, and the air smells of hung meat.”

5. The Larder

4 Surplus Choice
“Elk and aurochs, whole, stacked like firewood against the back wall and frozen hard. Four of them. Eight, if you count the ones further in where the light does not reach. This is nobody’s hunting. This is somebody’s pantry.”
  • Four carcasses within reach: a winter’s worth of Surplus, already killed, already frozen. Four sleds and a day’s hauling.
  • Obviously a hoard. Aurochs don’t come down from the Steplands in winter and nothing in this cave killed them. Seek Insight: “Who’s really in control here?” is the question that pays.
  • Take it and go: possible. Nothing stops them in this room. See Ways out, Rob the larder: the Bear’s winter comes to the village.
  • The cave keeps going. It shouldn’t; the bluff isn’t that deep. Past the meat the floor is swept and there is a small voice counting.
“…eleven, twelve… and the two the lady lost… fourteen. Oh. Oh, you are not the lady.”

6. The Tally-Keeper

Talk Debt
“A thing the height of a child, made of frost and twigs and a great deal of dignity, with a notched stick longer than itself. It stops counting, bows very low, and says: ‘The lady speaks of you. Will you sit? There is a fire.’”

The Tally-Keeper

Solitary, small, fae, meek, exact

HP 6; Armor 4 (resilience), 0 vs iron
Damage none; it has never hit anything
Special qualities Fae nature; cannot miscount; flees, never fights
Instinct to keep the count right

  • Offer food and a fire
  • Name what is owed, to the item
  • Run to tell the lady, or the lady’s rival
  • The fire and the food are real and warm and very welcome, and accepting them is a debt (Debts and promises). Say so plainly once the first hand reaches for it; the Fae do not hide this, they rely on people not asking.
  • It knows the count: fourteen carcasses, the Bear’s. It knows Hush is down two already, which it has not told anyone. Persuade with something it wants (to be right) and it says who the two went to: the trapper, and the cub.
  • Threaten it: it is gone, and it tells the first of the retinue it finds. Roll the Die of Fate: odd, Hush; even, Pale-as-Milk (7).
  • Iron shown: it stops bowing and starts backing. It will still answer, from further away.
“‘She is through there. She has been waiting since the snow came. Mind the lady. She is not as she was.’”

7. Hush-of-Snow

Talk Debt Rival
“She is glad to see you, and that is the worst of it. She is tall and white and thin, and the Codex is open on her knee with its copper plates frosted shut, and she has not been sleeping.”

Hush-of-Snow

Solitary, large, fae, magical, devious, hoarder; of the Bear of Winter’s retinue

HP 22; Armor 4 (resilience, ice), 0 vs iron
Damage grab (reach, forceful, grabby)
Special qualities Fae nature; never strikes first; carries the Hec’tumel Codex
Instinct to be owed

  • Weave snow and glamour
  • Lose count of something, for a friend
  • Be somewhere else

Her offer, first: the meat is the Bear’s, not hers, but she will lose count of four carcasses for friends. No price named. That is the price.

Her ask, second: Hec’tumel talks to her through the Codex every night, and she cannot put it down, because what is freely given cannot be given back. She wants to know how a mortal stops hearing it. If a PC has ever shed an arcanum of the Things Below, she knows, and she asks them by name. What they tell her is theirs to decide. What is true on her side: the Pale Serpent is working on her the way it works on everyone, and a Fae cannot die of it.

  • Hush’s ladder. Each time they take her help, refuse her, or insult her, move one step and tell them where they are: (1) charmed → (2) owed → (3) impatient → (4) offended. She starts at 1 and the meat offer does not move it; she means it as a gift.
  • Tell her how: she is grateful in the way that makes Fae dangerous, and asks for help with the rival. Owed, step 2, in the party’s favour for once.
  • Refuse her: step 3. She keeps the offer of the meat open anyway, which is how you know she is in trouble.
  • Offer to take the Codex: she cannot give it; it was given. She can lose it, if somebody else is holding it when the count is made. That somebody is then holding the Hec’tumel Codex.
  • Iron in hand: she will not be threatened. She leaves, and the four carcasses are the Bear’s again.

Pale-as-Milk, watching

“Something in the dark behind the meat has been standing very still for a long time, and it is smiling.”

Pale-as-Milk

Solitary, fae, magical, stealthy, spiteful; of the Bear of Winter’s retinue

HP 14; Armor 4 (resilience), 0 vs iron
Damage frost touch (hand, grabby; mark cold)
Special qualities Fae nature; unseen in snow and shadow until it chooses
Instinct to rise by another’s fall

  • Watch, and be seen watching
  • Name the count out loud
  • Run to tell the Bear
  • It wants Hush caught, not the party hurt. It will let four carcasses go out the door if it can tell the Bear who let them.
  • Drive it off: iron, or Defy Danger / Clash to corner it. Hurt by iron it flees for good, and tells no one.
  • Bribe it: it takes a promise, not a thing. “Say you will owe me” is enough, and then they do.
  • Let it go: it goes straight to the Bear. 8 happens at the cave mouth, on the way out, with the sleds loaded.
  • The trapper’s iron trap (2), set in the dark behind the meat, is the one way to catch a Fae that nobody is quick enough to touch.

Dialog (pick 2)

(Hush) “Four. I can lose four. I have lost two already and nobody has said. Take them with my love and do not thank me; thanking is a thing you owe.”
(Hush) “It talks at night. It knows my name, which it should not, and it knows yours. How does one stop hearing it?”
(Hush) “I cannot give it back. Giving is one way. You know that.”
(Pale-as-Milk) “Fourteen. Say it with me, sister. Fourteen, and four walking out the door. He will want to know who counted.”
(the Codex, if anyone touches it) “Yesss. Tell her. Tell her how, and I will tell you where the ice-woman keeps the thing she fears.”

8. The Bear

Only if Pale-as-Milk gets away Chieftain
“A scarred old man is sitting on the kerb of snow at the cave mouth with his hands between his knees. Sleet is falling in a circle around him, and nowhere else. He does not look at you. He says, ‘Hush. What are you holding that I am not?’”

The Bear of Winter

Solitary, huge, fae, magical, hoarder, devious, fierce, terrifying

HP 26; Armor 5 (resilience, ice), 2 vs iron
Damage bite or maul (close, hand, reach, forceful, grabby, messy, 1 piercing)
Special qualities Fae nature; immune to cold; sleet rises with his mood
Instinct to protect his status and holdings

  • Take the form of a scarred old man, or a storm of sleet
  • Call in a debt, in front of everyone
  • Send a minion to do his bidding

He has not come for the meat. He has come because one of his owes him and hid it. He asks Hush one question and then he waits, and the party decides whether to stand by her. He is a chieftain; he does not fight to the death and he does not need to.

  • Stand by her: say the four were a gift to friends and the friends will answer for it. He takes that; a debt moved to a mortal is still a debt. The village now owes the Bear of Winter four carcasses’ worth, and he will name the day. Hush is kept.
  • Say nothing: he takes Hush and the Codex both, and the party keeps the sleds. Hush does not come back. Hec’tumel has a chieftain’s ear now.
  • Hand over what she fears: the Codex, if someone is holding it. “What are you holding that I am not” was never about meat. He takes it, and he is pleased, and a pleased chieftain of the Fae is a threat to write up.
  • Fight: iron bites, black iron worse. He breaks a sled with the first blow to show them the shape of it. At 13+ damage taken he becomes sleet and is gone, and he is owed, and he knows the way to the village as well as she does.

Dialog (pick 1)

“Fourteen in, four out. I did not ask who took them. I asked what you are holding.”
(to the party) “Someone gave her that. Freely. I am told you are generous people. Be generous again.”
“Keep your meat. Meat is cheap. Say who owes me for it and we are done here, and it is a long cold way home for people who do not say.”

Ways out

  • Help Hush. Tell her what she asks and deal with Pale-as-Milk. The 4 Surplus is payment; nothing is owed, and a Fae of the Bear’s retinue is a friend of the village. Hec’tumel now knows whoever answered her.
  • Take the favour. 4 Surplus now, and they owe Hush one. Step 2 on the ladder. She will name the day, and it will be a day something else is also asking something of them.
  • Hunt honestly. Kill the bull and the sow: 2 Surplus the hard way, two fights with d10+4 bears in the dark, and the cub orphaned. Nobody owes anybody.
  • Rob the larder. 4 Surplus, no words with anyone. The Bear’s winter comes to the village: the next Seasons Change roll is made with disadvantage, snow buries the switchback, and something with cloven hooves is heard on the Ringwall at night.

The way home

Wolves
“Four sleds of frozen meat, dusk, and the bluff on your left. Behind you, where you cannot quite see, something is keeping pace. Then it is two somethings. Then you can count them.”

A winter pack, drawn by the meat. Run it as a chase, not a fight: Defy Danger to keep the sleds moving, Clash if they turn to hold the rear. The wolves want a carcass, not a person. If a PC fears wolves, or carries a fire that is proud of itself, this is their scene.

Winter Wolves (6)

Group, stealthy, organized, hungry

HP 6; Armor 1 (fur)
Damage bite (hand, grabby)
Special qualities take a straggler or a sled over a fight
Instinct to eat before the cold does

  • Cut a carcass loose: the pack stops. The village is 1 Surplus short.
  • Hold the rear with fire: the pack will not close on a flame. Three exchanges to the switchback. With the Flaming Sword lit, its bearer takes the Blaze die each exchange.
  • Someone flinches: don’t punish it; the fire does. The Blaze steps up on its own, and they have to decide whether to sheathe it in the dark with wolves behind.

Homefront

  • Surplus: one per sled that reaches the granary. Name the households that were going to go short, and who meets the sleds at the Ringwall.
  • The cub, if it came home: a bear in the village, and whose it is.
  • The trapper: a Marshedge body in the village’s ground. Someone in Marshedge will want to know, or will already know.
  • Hush knows the road to the village, and now knows how a mortal gets free of the Codex, or knows that they would not say. Either is a thread. The Codex is one owner from coming home.
  • Debts: write what is owed to whom on the threat tracker, by the item. The Fae will.
